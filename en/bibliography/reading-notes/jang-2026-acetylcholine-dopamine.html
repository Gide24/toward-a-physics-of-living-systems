Acetylcholine Demixes Heterogeneous Dopamine Signals for Learning and Moving

Reference

Jang, Hee Jae, et al. “Acetylcholine Demixes Heterogeneous Dopamine Signals for Learning and Moving.” Nature Neuroscience, vol. 29, Apr. 2026, pp. 840–850.

Personal summary

This article explores how the brain appears to differentiate signals related to learning from those related to movement. The authors show that dopamine does not always act in the same way: its effect depends on its temporal relationship with acetylcholine.

When dopamine appears shortly after specific decreases in acetylcholine activity, it seems to participate more strongly in learning and future behavioral modifications.

When dopamine appears in another temporal configuration, it seems more associated with movement vigor and movement execution.

The article therefore suggests that the brain does not process chemical signals only according to their nature, but also according to their temporal organization.

Relevance for the theoretical work

This article is particularly interesting because it reinforces the idea that the brain does not function as a static system in which one molecule has one unique function.

It introduces a more dynamic perspective in which context, temporality, and interactions between multiple systems must be considered simultaneously. Movement and learning appear here as dimensions that may not be entirely separable.

This article can be connected with the ideas developed on this website regarding the relationship between motor activity and memory, the influence of the environment on adaptive systems, and the way constraints may modify the organization of a living system.
